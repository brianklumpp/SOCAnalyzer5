Management of Citibank N.A.: 
Scope 
We have examined management of Citi Treasury and Trade Solutions’ (TTS) accompanying description of its 
“CitiDirect® and CitiConnect® Services System” (the System) for processing user entities’ transactions 
throughout the period October 1, 2023 to September 30, 2024 titled “Management of Citi TTS’ Description of 
its CitiDirect® and CitiConnect® Services System” (the Description) and the suitability of the design and operating 
effectiveness of the controls included in the Description to achieve the related control objectives stated in the 
Description, based on the criteria identified in “Management of Citi TTS’ Assertion” (the Assertion). The controls 
and control objectives included in the Description are those that management of TTS believes are likely to be 
relevant to user entities’ internal control over financial reporting, and the Description does not include those 
aspects of the System that are not likely to be relevant to user entities’ internal control over financial reporting. 
The information included in Section V, “Other Information Provided by Management of Citi TTS,” is presented 
by management of TTS to provide additional information and is not a part of the Description. Information about 
Citi Organizational Change, Business Continuity/Disaster Recovery, Anti-Money Laundering, Fraud 
Management, Privacy, and Disaster Recovery Procedures has not been subjected to the procedures applied in 
the examination of the Description and of the suitability of the design and operating effectiveness of controls 
to achieve the related control objectives stated in the Description and, accordingly, we express no opinion on 
it.  
TTS uses the subservice organizations identified in Section III to perform some of the services provided to user 
entities that are likely to be relevant to those user entities’ internal control over financial reporting. The 
Description includes only the control objectives and related controls of TTS and excludes the control objectives 
and related controls of the subservice organizations. The Description also indicates that certain control 
objectives specified by TTS can be achieved only if complementary subservice organization controls assumed in 
the design of TTS’ controls are suitably designed and operating effectively, along with the related controls at 
TTS. Our examination did not extend to controls of the subservice organizations, and we have not evaluated 
the suitability of the design or operating effectiveness of such complementary subservice organization controls. 
The Description indicates that certain control objectives specified in the Description can be achieved only if 
complementary user entity controls assumed in the design of TTS’ controls are suitably designed and operating 
effectively, along with related controls at TTS. Our examination did not extend to such complementary user 
entity controls, and we have not evaluated the suitability of the design or operating effectiveness of such 
complementary user entity controls.

Service Organization’s Responsibilities 
In Section 2, management of TTS has provided the Assertion about the fairness of the presentation of the 
Description and suitability of the design and operating effectiveness of the controls to achieve the related 
control objectives stated in the Description. TTS is responsible for preparing the Description and Assertion, 
including the completeness, accuracy, and method of presentation of the Description and Assertion, providing 
the services covered by the Description, specifying the control objectives and stating them in the Description, 
identifying the risks that threaten the achievement of the control objectives, selecting the criteria stated in the 
Assertion, and designing, implementing, and documenting controls that are suitably designed and operating 
effectively to achieve the related control objectives stated in the Description. 
Service Auditors’ Responsibilities 
Our responsibility is to express an opinion on the fairness of the presentation of the Description and on the 
suitability of the design and operating effectiveness of the controls to achieve the related control objectives 
stated in the Description, based on our examination.  
Our examination was conducted in accordance with attestation standards established by the American Institute 
of Certified Public Accountants. Those standards require that we plan and perform the examination to obtain 
reasonable assurance about whether, in all material respects, based on the criteria in the Assertion, the 
Description is fairly presented, and the controls were suitably designed and operated effectively to achieve the 
related control objectives stated in the Description throughout the period October 1, 2023 to September 30, 
2024. We believe that the evidence we obtained is sufficient and appropriate to provide a reasonable basis for 
our opinion. 
An examination of a description of a service organization’s system and the suitability of the design and operating 
effectiveness of controls involves: 
•
performing procedures to obtain evidence about the fairness of the presentation of the description and the
suitability of the design and operating effectiveness of the controls to achieve the related control objectives
stated in the description, based on the criteria in management’s assertion,
•
assessing the risks that the description is not fairly presented and that the controls were not suitably
designed or operating effectively to achieve the related control objectives stated in the description,
•
testing the operating effectiveness of those controls that management considers necessary to provide
reasonable assurance that the related control objectives stated in the description were achieved
•
evaluating the overall presentation of the description, suitability of the control objectives stated in the
description, and suitability of the criteria specified by the service organization in its assertion.
We are required to be independent and to meet our other ethical responsibilities in accordance with relevant 
ethical requirements relating to the examination engagement.

Inherent Limitations 
The Description is prepared to meet the common needs of a broad range of user entities and their auditors who 
audit and report on user entities’ financial statements and may not, therefore, include every aspect of the 
System that each individual user entity may consider important in its own particular environment. Because of 
their nature, controls at a service organization may not prevent, or detect and correct, all misstatements in 
processing or reporting transactions. Also, the projection to the future of any evaluation of the fairness of the 
presentation of the Description, or conclusions about the suitability of the design or operating effectiveness of 
the controls to achieve the related control objectives stated in the Description, is subject to the risk that controls 
at a service organization may become ineffective.

Overview of Applications 
CitiDirect® 
CitiDirect® formerly known as CitiDirect BE is a reporting and transaction-initiator, user entity-delivery tool 
that combines various banking functions together on one platform. CitiDirect BE is the legacy platform 
that was transitioned to CitiDirect® as of April 30, 2024. CitiDirect interfaces with Citi’s products and 
services in a growing list of countries and provides real-time global access to user entities’ accounts. 
CitiDirect® serves as a communication vehicle and allows its user entities to initiate, translate and transmit 
transactions to the appropriate backend systems. 
CitiDirect® provides a single-entry portal offering a contemporary user interface that integrates the 
existing CitiDirect® transactional and self-service capabilities. The portal also provides new analytic 
applications such as trend analytics scorecards, self-service tools, and multimedia business intelligence. 
Client Administration Service (CAS) is the central, on-boarding user entity and user-management tool for 
CitiDirect®. CAS allows for the creation and management of user entities and users and maintains 
high-level entitlements.  
CitiDirect® delivers banking applications to a user entity desktop and allows users to access and control 
their banking activities at every level of their organization. CitiDirect® safeguards critical data behind 
parallel security processes using encryption technologies. CitiDirect® uses multi-layered security including 
public and private access keys with user IDs and multiple authorization controls. Figure 1.4 illustrates the 
flow of data and transactions between application users and backend processing systems. The scope of 
this report includes transaction initiation and reporting only. User entity systems and Citi backend 
processors are not in scope for this report. 
CitiDirect® Mobile 
CitiDirect® Mobile is an award-winning, mobile channel extension of Citi’s flagship online banking 
platform, CitiDirect®. It is designed to provide Treasury executives with the ability to securely manage their 
global cash and trade needs anytime, anywhere, real-time. This intuitive, web-browser-based application 
is device-agnostic, offering user entities convenient access to key functionalities right on their mobile 
device. CitiDirect® Mobile is available exclusively for CitiDirect® user entities and leverages the same 
security and entitlements infrastructure as CitiDirect®. With CitiDirect® Mobile, user entities can: 
• 
Manage payments on-the-go: Initiate, authorize and release payments 
• 
Gain visibility into global cash positions by viewing real-time intraday cash positions and running 
account and payment status inquiries 
• 
Receive real-time SMS/email notifications 
• 
Authorize a variety of trade transactions, including Import Letters of Credit (LC), Guarantees/Standby 
LCs, as well as Open Account and Collection Notifications 
• 
Users can download the CitiDirect Mobile APP from the main app stores and use it on their Tablet 
devices.

CitiDirect® SaaS

CitiConnect® 
CitiConnect® is the primary tool used for TTS’ global cash, payments, collections, and information network. 
CitiConnect® provides a secure global platform employing several different file and messages formats, 
transmission methods, and file security options. This allows user entities to send and receive files and 
messages associated with payments, debits, and status in a standard format through the CTSI user 
interface. 
Using CitiConnect®, user entities can process transactions through a single integrated channel providing 
them with greater visibility into their financial transactions. The wide range of connectivity capabilities 
within CitiConnect® allows for increased automation, reduced errors and straight through processing. 
CitiConnect® is composed of three main components: CitiConnect® for Files, CitiConnect® for SWIFT and 
CitiConnect® API. Figure 1.6 provides an overview of CitiConnect®: 
1. CitiConnect® for Files (CCF): is Citi’s global file delivery solution that translates industry standard 
market messages as well as customized formats, and dispatches high volume transactions to regional 
product processors for processing, bookkeeping and settlement. 
2. CitiConnect® for SWIFT (CCS): is Citi’s SWIFT-based solution that leverages the connectivity and 
infrastructure provided by the SWIFTNet to deliver messages.

Scope of the Report 
The applications in scope for this report are: CitiDirect v2 and CitiDirect v3 (including CitiDirect® Mobile 
and CitiDirect® SaaS) and CitiConnect®. The report covers the control objectives related to transaction 
initiation, translation, and reporting, as well as certain underlying general IT controls. The activities 
covered for each application in scope are listed within Table 1.0 below.  
Citi Treasury and Trade Solutions’ control objectives and related controls are included in Section IV of this 
report, “Citi Treasury and Trade Solutions’ Control Objectives and Related Controls, and KPMG LLP’s Tests 
of Controls and Results of Tests.” Although the control objectives and related controls are presented in 
Section IV, they are, nevertheless, an integral part of Citi Treasury and Trade Solutions’ description of its 
system as described in Section III. The initiation of transactions using the CitiDirect®, CitiDirect® Mobile 
and CitiDirect® SaaS applications is covered within the scope of this report. Transaction initiation activities 
supporting each of these applications are similar. They may differ in specific configuration required by the 
CitiDirect® SaaS user entity banks and, where applicable, they are separately described and documented 
in this section. Unless noted otherwise, the description is applicable to CitiDirect®, CitiDirect® Mobile, and 
CitiDirect® SaaS. 
This report also covers transaction initiation through CitiConnect®, including the three applications that 
compose the CitiConnect® suite: CitiConnect® for Files, CitiConnect® API and CitiConnect® for SWIFT. 
Payment transactions initiated through CitiConnect® are submitted by the user entities through files and 
messages. Therefore, transaction initiation through CitiConnect® related controls are covered under the 
Import control objectives. 
Due to Polish data privacy regulations, data from Bank Handlowy w Warszawie could not be accessed 
during the preparation of this report. For this reason, Poland employee entitlement modifications and 
Poland client activation activities were not reviewed as part of this SOC1.  
Additional services, indicated in the Overview of Organization and Operations section, offered by TTS that 
are not listed in this Scope section, are not covered by this report. 
As part of its overall controls reporting program, TTS management sets and determines the scope and 
timing of each report. This report features the services provided by TTS’ CitiDirect® (including CitiDirect® 
Mobile and CitiDirect® SaaS) and CitiConnect® services and focuses on control objectives as they may be 
relevant to the internal controls for financial reporting for TTS’ user entities. The scope of the report 
covers the business processes that TTS has determined are significant to its user entities from a financial 
reporting perspective and the applicable information technology and related processes specific to 
supporting the CitiDirect® (including CitiDirect® Mobile and CitiDirect® SaaS) and CitiConnect® services. TTS 
management is responsible for the identification of the control objectives, risks, and for the manual and 
automated controls placed into operation to achieve those objectives.

Assessment and Monitoring 
Control Environment 
TTS Control Environment represents the collective efforts to establish and enhance the effectiveness of 
specific controls. The control environment reflects the overall attitude, awareness and actions of the 
Board of Directors, management and others concerning the importance of controls and the emphasis 
within TTS. 
Organization and Management 
The philosophy and operating style of TTS emanates from senior management. The philosophy of senior 
management is to manage and control risk through a hierarchy of controls and management processes, 
which reinforce a “control culture.” These controls and management processes are geared towards 
identifying risks and managing those risks to acceptable levels. Ongoing changes in businesses, 
technology, and world financial markets and within TTS dictate that risk identification and the reduction 
of risk is a continuous part of normal management activities. 
Oversight is performed by: 
• 
Citigroup Audit Committee, which supervises the activities of Internal Audit (IA) department, and acts 
as the liaison for the External Auditors and the Regulatory Examiners; and 
• 
Global Compliance Risk Management, which monitors the results of the Management Controls 
Assessment (MCA) reviews performed by each operating unit. Risk Management also monitors IA and 
regulatory agency reports, performs risk analyses, monitors corrective action plans, and issues control 
summary reports to management. 
Management has formal information and reporting systems, which are used to monitor the activities 
within each business. Citi has developed a policy and a series of internal controls that are designed to 
identify risks in each of its businesses and to provide reasonable assurance that procedures are developed 
and implemented to contain these risks. 
The MCA is the local business’ process followed by Citi’s businesses to monitor and report risks, mitigating 
controls and compliance with internal policies. TTS embraced the MCA process at its inception and 
participates in rigorous independent challenge reviews and IA reviews of its MCA.

The entire Citi organization aspires to the highest standards of ethical conduct, as it performs services

Significant Policies and Practices 
Citi has adopted and follows the Committee of Sponsoring Organizations of the Treadway Commission 
(COSO) framework for internal controls. The original framework issued in 1992 was updated by COSO to 
accommodate for increased complexity and use of technology within organizations. The five components 
of the framework remain the same, and an additional layer consisting of seventeen principles is added to 
provide additional guidance and specificity applicable to each component. 
Citi launched an enterprise-wide program in 2013 to identify the requirements of the updated framework 
and to implement, where necessary, measures to link current control elements to the individual 
principles. Management, including risk management teams across the Citi businesses and functions, 
identified key elements and metrics that can be mapped to individual principles and are building 
mechanisms to demonstrate compliance with the more granular requirements. This report currently 
addresses the five framework components consistent with the 1992 internal controls standard, with the 
relevant principles from the 2013 revision discussed where appropriate. 
Information technology at Citigroup is managed and governed by three broad policies. The Citi 
Information Security Standard (CISS) links the requirements of the International Organization for 
Standardization (ISO) 27002 standard to the strategic objectives set forth by the Citigroup Chief 
Information Officer Council and defines the policies, roles, and responsibilities for management of 
information security across the Citigroup enterprise. The CISS provides the minimum security and 
information protection requirements for Citi businesses to be compliant with leading information security 
practices and local and global regulatory expectations. Individual functional groups may adopt additional 
policies for management of information security at the functional level, provided that these policies meet 
or exceed the CISS requirements.

Management Controls Assessment 
Operational risk is the risk of loss from inadequate or failed internal, manual and/or automated systems 
processes. The Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (the Basel Accord) has set out specific 
requirements on banks’ group wide internal risk management control system, recordkeeping, and 
periodic reporting. The Sarbanes Oxley Act of 2002 (SOX) Section 404 outlines management’s 
responsibility for reporting on internal controls over financial reporting and disclosures, requiring a 
quarterly evaluation of the quality of internal controls over financial reporting and disclosures. To ensure 
that Citi complies with the required internal control review and assessment process, the company has 
adopted uniform Management Controls Assessment standards and procedures which include the 
implementation and management of a full-blown periodic exercise of identifying key processes, significant 
risks, controls to manage those risks, and methods to monitor that the controls are functioning as 
expected. 
Monitoring 
Citi recognizes the need for internal control systems to be monitored to ensure the quality of the systems’ 
performance is maintained. This is accomplished through ongoing monitoring activities, point in time 
evaluations, and/or a combination of the two. Ongoing monitoring occurs during operations and includes 
regular management and supervisory activities as well as the actions of personnel while performing their 
duties. The scope and frequency of separate evaluations depend primarily on an assessment of risks and 
the effectiveness of ongoing monitoring procedures. Upon discovery in the monitoring process, internal 
control deficiencies are reported to management. Serious matters are immediately reported to senior 
management and the Board. 
Monitoring activities at Citi are closely related to the Risk Assessment process described above and include 
the monitoring of risk and ensuring the integrity of the control activities is conducted via a formal 
self-testing program that culminates in a documented MCA. The corresponding corrective action plans 
(CAP) are escalated to upper management for monitoring and action. Separate, point in time evaluations, 
in the form of the quarterly self-assessment process, encompasses an end-to-end evaluation process that 
monitors key processes TTS provides for user entity organizations. The formal CAP mechanism captures, 
and reports identified internal control issues, allowing management to track and manage the issues raised 
by the self-assessment exercise. Ongoing monitoring activities such as Audit Committee oversight and the 
Internal Audit unit’s independent reporting of the internal audit function provide additional monitoring 
mechanisms within the organization. In addition, the MCA process is evaluated by Internal Audit and O&T 
Risk Management for compliance with company policies and procedures.

Payments 
CitiDirect®’s payment capabilities have been developed in line with local clearing systems from around the 
world and provide secure domestic and cross border transaction initiation. Within the United States, 
free-format and pre-formatted payment methods include Fedwire, CHIPS, book, ACH credit, ACH debit,

the status of payments at any time and can make online repairs if necessary. CitiDirect® Mobile user 
entities can initiate, authorize, and release payments based on their entitlements.  
The system also incorporates user entities’-controlled disbursement data with information about their

online notifications of the current day’s clearings. The disbursement funding notification screen displays 
the first and second presentment items and dollar amount subtotals for specific accounts that day. 
CitiDirect® also provides the capability to initiate multicurrency payments by wire transfer from over 90 
currencies to almost any location in the world. The user entity can use a variety of reporting and initiation 
options to determine their net position, to reduce payment amounts and volume, to minimize float, FX 
costs and transaction fees, and to pay and receive from a central point in local currencies. 
In the following regions and for the following payment types, CitiDirect® has default “Duplicate Payment 
Check” functionality over payments whose transaction reference numbers were manually assigned by the

• 
USA: Book Transfer and Foreign Currency Fund Transfer. 
• 
Canada and Asia (excluding India, Bangladesh, and Sri Lanka): Book transfer, Domestic Fund Transfer, 
Cross Border Fund Transfer. 
The duplicate payment check is performed on a submitted payment by comparing the following payment 
attributes with previously initiated payments: 
• 
The transaction reference number, 
• 
The value date and 
• 
The user entity’s debit account 
CitiDirect® will reject a payment if it identifies a previously initiated payment with the same attributes as 
listed above.

Implementation 
Implementation is the effort involved in setting up user entities on the CitiDirect® and CitiConnect® 
applications and making changes requested by user entities on existing setups. CitiDirect® Mobile is a 
channel extension of CitiDirect® and as such follows the CitiDirect® implementation process. Pictorially 
detailed in Figure 1.7 key tasks include data gathering, formal solution configuration request by the user 
entity, and implementation quality reviews. The implementation follows a lifecycle to verify that 
information is exchanged accurately and in accordance with user entity expectations. This lifecycle is 
integrated into the Customer Relationship Management workflow tool through which the user entity’s 
requests are monitored. Processes include the completion and signing of the intake form to establish the 
user entity organization as a user entity. This form contains information about Security Managers 
designated by the user entities, description of the solution package to be implemented, debit and credit 
accounts, legal entities, standardized user access profiles, and flow controls settings.  
After the intake form has been properly prepared and authorized by the user entity, the deal is assigned 
to the implementations team who in turn engage the activation team to set up the solution package. The 
activation team builds the package and performs quality reviews on the constructed solution to determine 
whether the user entity’s specifications were properly configured. Upon completion of the solution, the 
implementations team is notified to integrate and coordinate testing, support, and training activities with 
the user entity.

To protect transactions and information and to comply with legal and regulatory guidelines, CitiDirect®

Define Flow Maintenance 
The levels of authorization required for transactions and services processing are defined by the security

The default configuration for flow control is:

configured.

Release is the last step in the processing cycle. 
3) Verification Required – Yes. The transaction is required to be verified by another user to be processed. 
This step can occur if the payment results in an error or warning, and if required by the user entity.

Transaction Instruction Validation 
Transactions are processed by CitiDirect® according to the instructions entered manually by the user or 
imported in files using the file import utility1. For manually initiated transactions, instructions are 
validated for completeness by verifying that required data fields are populated. The fields required to be 
populated are dependent on the type of transaction. If a required field has not been populated, a 
notification message indicates the error(s) to the user and the transaction(s) is not processed until 
repaired. CitiDirect® also performs validation on the date and time of the payment transaction to 
determine compliance with the corresponding cut-off time. The cut-off time is the latest time within a day 
or week when a user entity-initiated transaction will be accepted for processing. 
Instructions, uploaded into CitiDirect® and CitiDirect® SaaS via the file import utility, are secured using 
digital signatures. Digital signatures are used to detect alteration of data during transmission or storage. 
Files modified after they were digitally signed are aborted and an error message is presented to the user. 
Transactions that pass the checks are processed through CitiDirect® in accordance with the flow control 
set by the security administrator. The status of the transactions is updated based on the step of the flow 
control the transaction is in. Users are informed of the status of their transactions through summary 
screens and through notification messages alerting them to the next step. 
Transaction Mapping and Transmission 
After a transaction has been released in CitiDirect®, user entity transaction instructions are mapped and 
translated into standard SWIFT messages and are sent to the backend systems for processing. The 
mapping is created in accordance with the user entity preferences set during the implementation process. 
Using these mapping tables, CitiDirect® builds and sends the corresponding SWIFT messages to Citi’s 
backend systems for processing. CitiDirect® SaaS sends the message to the user entity bank’s backend 
systems. 
For all transactions initiated within CitiDirect®, an acknowledgement message is returned from the 
backend systems with the status of transactions processed and a transaction reference number. The 
transaction reference number is an identifier assigned to every transaction initiated within CitiDirect®. It 
is unique for each user entity, per branch and date. For messages unsuccessfully processed, the 
acknowledgement message contains an error code. The error code would need to be corrected by either 
the end user or through Citigroup’s incident management process, depending on the type of error code 
received. '

CitiDirect®, CitiDirect Mobile® and CitiDirect® SaaS 
CitiDirect® enables the user entity to securely upload and download data files via the internet through TLS, 
with at least 256-bit encryption. Supported import files include payment, trade, and multicurrency 
instructions. Data validation routines are embedded within the applications to validate the integrity of the 
contents of the file submitted. The file import and export functionality are based on entitlements that 
restrict, so that only authorized individuals can import and export data. 
Import and Export profiles are sets of rules and maps which define the security method to be applied to 
import and export files. Users with the appropriate entitlements to CitiDirect® Mobile may authorize file 
imports. CitiDirect® has the capability to import and export files using the S/MIME protocol for 
authentication and privacy, and cyclic redundancy checks (CRC) for integrity. A CRC can be used in the 
same way as a checksum to detect alteration of data during transmission or storage. S/MIME protocol 
provides cryptographic security services for authentication of the source and the receiver, message 
integrity and non-repudiation of origin, and privacy and data security (using encryption). When the signed 
file is received by CitiDirect®, signature validation is performed to determine the following: 
• 
File contents have not been altered after the file was signed; 
• 
Signature on the file matches the certificate selected by the user in the delivery option for the job; 
and 
• 
Certificate has not expired. 
CitiDirect® provides users with an Automated File and Report Delivery (AFRD) utility a way to automate 
the end-to-end File Import and Export processes. The AFRD utility performs the previously mentioned 
security functions according to the S/MIME industry specification for encryption/decryption and digital 
signature processing. The AFRD utility creates acknowledgement files to confirm that CitiDirect® 
successfully retrieved the import file. Scheduled imports that did not run, and errors detected during the 
file retrieval are listed in the file import inquiry screen.

CitiConnect® for Files 
CitiConnect® for Files enables the user entity to initiate transactions by transmitting data files via the 
internet. The transaction flow within CitiConnect® is as follows: User entities backend systems generate a 
transaction initiation (TI) file containing payments and/or collections transactions. The TI file is encrypted 
and digitally signed using a security tool installed in the user entities systems during implementation. The 
encrypted TI file is transmitted to Citi. CitiConnect® periodically checks the files received against the 
preconfigured schedule of expected file receipts specified by each user entity. If a file has not been 
received within the expected window, an e-mail notification is sent to the user entity. 
Upon receipt of a file, CitiConnect® authenticates the file submission based on its name and the file’s 
receiving location. This corresponds to a predefined File Profile that is set up in CitiConnect®, which also 
determines how to process the file. After successful identification, CitiConnect® performs a duplicate file 
check to determine whether the file received has already been processed, by comparing the name and 
size of the file to other files received from the same user entity over the last 90 days. If a suspected 
duplicate is found, file processing is stopped, and the user entity is notified via e-mail. 
Otherwise, files are then checked to determine whether they are compliant with the predefined file 
format, syntax, and message content. For each transaction within the file, CitiConnect® performs the 
following checks and activities: 
• 
Verifies the inclusion of mandatory elements, each record length, order of records, start- and 
end-position of all fields, and any specific field format requirements (e.g., date-time fields are in 
correct format);

• 
Validates the transaction references is within the list of accounts that were specified for each 
Customer File Profile; and 
• 
Verifies whether there is a valid payment/collection instrument type specified for the Customer File 
Profile. 
Files delivered to Citi utilize a File Authorization control through the CitiConnect® browser. This is 
designated at the file profile level. Appropriately entitled users in each user entity organization login to 
the CitiConnect® browser and either approve or reject the file. If subsequent validation of the initial user’s 
action is required, then the appropriately entitled users also login to CitiConnect® to review the request

If the file is rejected, file processing ceases. 
Upon receipt of transactions, Citi’s backend systems send an acknowledgement to CitiConnect®.

CitiConnect® for inclusion in response files, for viewing on the browser, or a file processing confirmation 
fax. 
CitiConnect® for SWIFT 
CitiConnect® for SWIFT provides connectivity to user entities through the SWIFT network. Incoming 
messages are checked against the user entity and message type entitlements. If an incoming message 
does not pass these checks, CitiConnect® generates an acknowledgement containing an error message 
and sends it back to the sender. The messages which have been successfully validated for user entity 
entitlements are transmitted to the backend systems for processing.

Encryption – Data encryption protects data from access by unauthorized users. To allow user entities

– 
Challenge Response Token: Either (i) a mobile application based soft token (e.g., Mobile Token) or

multifactor authentication 
– 
Biometrics: A digital authentication method that utilizes a user’s unique physical traits, (such as a 
fingerprint and facial recognition), built-in biometric technology on the user’s mobile device, and 
cryptographic techniques to gain access to CitiDirect®. Physical trait data is not transferred to the 
Bank when the user selects this authentication method.

Vulnerability Assessment 
On a periodic basis, as well as whenever significant development occurs, internet facing applications, 
including CitiDirect®, CitiDirect® Mobile, and CitiConnect® are subject to vulnerability assessments. The 
Application Vulnerability Assessment (AVA) is an independent assessment of vulnerabilities and/or 
potential weaknesses in an application provided to the business sponsoring the application. This 
assessment looks for defects in the construction, logic, or assembly of the application that may lead to a 
loss of confidentiality, integrity, or availability of the application. 
CitiDirect®, CitiDirect® Mobile, and CitiConnect® are subject to internal and external Application 
Vulnerability Assessments: 
• 
Internal Vulnerability Assessment (IVA) – Security testing of a web-based application using a 
vulnerability assessment procedure approved by Citi’s Information Security Technology Group (ISTG). 
This assessment is composed of manual tests and automated vulnerability scanning tools. 
• 
External Vulnerability Assessment (EVA) – Security testing by an ISTG approved vendor, of a 
web-based application, product, or service’s functionality prior to a new product or existing enhanced 
product deployment. It is performed using an ISTG approved vulnerability assessment procedure and 
composed of manual tests, automated vulnerability scanning tools, or additional test protocols 
developed by the vendors. 
The applicability, frequency and extent of the application vulnerability assessment process depend on the 
type of changes made to the applications, as well as the security impact of the changes. 
Types of Changes to the Applications 
1. Cosmetic or Static Content Changes – Changes affecting the presentation of content to the end-users. 
These types of changes have generally minimal or no security impact and therefore rarely require a 
vulnerability assessment (VA). 
2. Hosting Environment Changes – Changes to Internet hosting facilities or infrastructure 
technologies/products that are used to support the application. These types of changes rarely require 
a VA at the application level; however, they may require an assessment on the infrastructure. 
3. Security Changes – Changes that affect the implementation of security controls within the application, 
including changes to application login/authentication processes, security session management, access 
control or entitlement modules, cryptographic algorithms or routines, security protocols, and use of 
restricted technologies. These changes usually have significant security impact and therefore must 
undergo a VA.

4. Program Logic Change – Code changes affecting the business functionality such as changes to business 
logic, search engines, new external system interfaces and communications with external providers. 
These changes usually have significant security impact and therefore must undergo a VA. 
Security Impact Level 
The determination on the impact of the change on the Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability and/or 
security architecture of the application of the Information Assets being changed must be made by an ISO 
or an independent security specialist. The Security Impact Level is documented in the application change 
documentation and is classified as: 
1. Major – Significant impact to the security state of the application that require the highest level of 
security assurance. This requires a full or partial retest using the required VA method for the 
application. 
2. Minor – Moderate impact on the security state of the application that requires a level of security 
assurance less than that for major changes. This requires a full or partial retest using a VA method 
designated by the ISO. 
3 
No Impact – Minimal or no impact to the security state of the application that does not warrant any 
security assurance. There is no VA requirement for “No Impact” changes.

Business Continuity Plan 
Treasury and Trade Solutions maintain a fully documented comprehensive contingency plan to ensure 
continuity of business in the event of a business disruption. A fully functional contingency site is available 
and is tested annually. 
Business continuity planning has been implemented to maintain a business-as-usual presence in the event 
of a crisis or business interruption. 
Citi’s Continuity of Business (COB) Plan identifies the actions required to maintain the critical operational 
processes functioning, system access requirements, alternative back-up sites, call trees, equipment and 
personnel requirements, and other logistical requirements. The COB Plan is tested at least annually, 
evaluated, and approved by management. 
Responsibility 
Each Business Unit is accountable for its COB plan and recovery of critical operational functions and is 
required to develop and maintain their COB plan. This is reviewed by Citi’s Internal Audit department (IA). 
Plan Maintenance Schedule 
Each Business Plan is reviewed and revised on a quarterly basis to reflect improvements or updates in the 
recovery program. 
Testing 
A full-fledged contingency drill is practiced annually, which includes running Production traffic on COB 
infrastructure for one week to ensure sufficient capacity exists in COB environments to handle normal 
Production volumes Citi’s IA annually reviews both the contingency plan, and the overall performance 
during the drill. 
Annual Certification Process 
Annually, the Business Unit Manager must sign-off attesting that the Business Unit: 
• 
Identified critical functions and vital records; 
• 
Identified high-risk functions along with any outstanding corrective action plans and reasonable target 
dates for closure; 
• 
Identified the impact and risk to the institution of a loss or interruption of critical information systems; 
• 
Business functions have been assessed according to the COB policy; and 
• 
Implemented a viable, workable contingency plan, which is documented and tested.
