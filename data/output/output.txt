OVERVIEW OF OPERATIONS 
 
Company Background 
 
Anaqua, Inc. (the “Company”) provides software and services through a web-based unified platform for intellectual 
property (IP) – a comprehensive software platform capable of managing and automating the full IP lifecycle of 
patents, trademarks, designs, trade secrets and contracts.  Today, Anaqua’s AQX software is used by nearly a 
million IP executives, attorneys, paralegals and administrators in corporations and law firms all over the world. 
  
Anaqua has more than doubled in size over the past five years through organic growth, merging with Lecorpio, a 
leading IP management software solutions company, and acquiring ideaPoint, an innovation management solution 
provider and AcclaimIP, the patent analytics software solution. 
  
In June 2020, Anaqua acquired IP management software and practice automation provider O P Solutions.  The 
acquisition accelerated Anaqua’s law firm growth strategy and supported the company’s commitment to delivering 
IP solutions that address the diversified and evolving needs of today’s IP management market.  O P Solutions’ 
flagship product PATTSY WAVE® joined AQX Law Firms as a second distinct brand to help address the varying 
needs and requirements of the IP law firm market. 
  
Anaqua was founded in 2004 and is headquartered in Boston, Massachusetts, with additional locations in the U.S. 
as well as Europe and Asia.

Infrastructure and Software 
 
The Intellectual Asset Management System is hosted within the IBM and Microsoft Azure infrastructure. Anaqua 
does not own or maintain hardware located in the IBM and Microsoft Azure data centers.  Anaqua operates under 
a shared security responsibility model, where IBM and Microsoft Azure are responsible for the security of the 
underlying cloud infrastructure (i.e., physical infrastructure, geographical regions, availability zones, edge locations, 
operating, managing and controlling the components from the host operating system, virtualization layer and 
storage) and Anaqua is responsible for securing the application platform deployed in the IBM and Microsoft Azure 
Cloud Management Consoles (i.e., applications, identity access management, operating system and network 
firewall configurations, server-side encryption).  Production servers and client facing applications are logically and 
physically secured from Anaqua’s internal corporate information systems. 
  
The production network is administered remotely; access to the production network requires an established Virtual 
Private Network (VPN) and remote desktop protocol (RDP) connection authenticated via two-factor authentication 
(2FA).  Access to the public facing applications within the Intellectual Asset Management System is encrypted via 
TLS to help ensure a secure transmission over the Internet for the platform.  In addition, firewalls are utilized to 
restrict access to the production platform.

Procedures 
 
Access, Authentication, and Authorization 
 
Documented security policies and procedures are in place regarding system settings, authentication, access 
control, security monitoring, and password protection. 
  
Access to system information, including confidential customer information, is protected by multiple authentication 
layers.  In order to connect to the Intellectual Asset Management System production systems, Anaqua personnel 
are required to first connect to the encrypted VPN system, via a username and password integrated with AD when 
accessing the IBM or Microsoft Azure production environments.  Access to the VPN must be administered by IBM 
as part of a managed hosting agreement purchased by Anaqua with approval from the chief information officer 
(CIO) or the chief executive officer (CEO).  Access and authentication to the VPN for the Azure production 
environment is controlled by Anaqua.  Once connected to the VPN, Anaqua personnel can authenticate to the IBM 
and Azure production domains, application servers, and databases through RDP connection via a username and 
password along with 2FA.  Authentication configurations to the IBM Cloud Management Console is managed by 
IBM.  Anaqua personnel authenticate to the IBM and Microsoft Azure Cloud Management Consoles via a username 
and password, along with 2FA, as set by Anaqua.  The in-scope systems are configured to authenticate users with 
a unique user account and enforce minimum password requirements.  
  
Access to the front-end applications requires a unique user account using an e-mail address and enforce minimum 
password requirements.  The front-end applications are configured with baseline password settings that can then 
be customized by customers for their instance.  Furthermore, privileged access to in-scope systems is restricted to 
authorized personnel.  
  
Access Requests and Access Revocation 
A process has been established by Anaqua to manage user access requests, modifications, and deletions.  The 
process varies based on the type of access needed.  The corporate IT team is responsible for assigning and 
maintaining access rights to the corporate systems based on the individual’s role and business justification.  User 
provisioning is requested in the HR onboarding form and attached to a Zendesk ticket.  The IT department will 
provision access in AD as per the requested permissions of the onboarding form attached to the associated 
Zendesk ticket.  Anaqua personnel are set up with an AD account allowing them to access the systems and 
resources.  Users requiring access to the IBM and Azure production networks are requested in the Zendesk ticketing 
system and require approval from the CIO or the CEO.  Access requests to the IBM production environment are 
then routed to IBM for provisioning as part of the managed hosting agreement with IBM.  Access requests to the 
Azure production environment are controlled by Anaqua and Zendesk tickets are routed to management for 
approvals.  In the event of a termination, HR personnel will notify corporate IT for access revocation via a termination 
ticket.  In addition, to help ensure access privileges in the production network are commensurate with job 
responsibilities, the senior director of hosting operations will perform a formal review of system access rights on a 
quarterly basis.  If any individual is deemed to have inappropriate access, a separate request is raised with IBM to 
revoke access.  A ticket would be raised and handled internally by Anaqua personnel if any individual is deemed to 
have inappropriate access to the production environment. 
  
Access reviews are conducted annually as part of Anaqua’s annual risk assessment process.  Customers are 
responsible for provisioning access to their instance of the Intellectual Asset Management System products, as well 
as configure their own password rules. 
  
Network and Data Security 
A firewall is in place to filter unauthorized inbound network traffic from the internet.  Information involved in 
application services passing over public networks is protected from fraudulent activity, contract dispute and 
unauthorized disclosure and modification through the use of firewalls and encryption methods.  Encryption 
technologies or secured communication channels are used to protect transmission of data and other 
communications beyond connectivity access points.  An encrypted VPN is required for remote access to the 
production environment to help ensure the security and integrity of the data passing over the public network.  A 
policy is in place to guide personnel on the use and protection of cryptographic keys to protect the confidentiality, 
authenticity, and/or integrity of information.  Groups of information services, users and information systems are 
segregated on networks.  Furthermore, an inventory of system assets and components is maintained to classify 
and manage the information assets.

To further protect the network, antivirus software is installed on production systems to detect and prevent the 
transmission of data or files that contain certain virus signatures recognized by the antivirus software.  In addition,

Change Management 
Documented policies and procedures are in place to guide personnel in the change management process which 
includes documentation, testing, and approval by the quality assurance (QA) team.  The change management 
procedures are updated regularly and made accessible to employees in a shared location.  Infrastructure changes 
are documented in a ticketing system and require approval from IT management.  Product changes are documented 
and tracked in a ticketing system and performed by the engineering team.  Once developed, the change is tested 
by the QA team and if approved, the change is merged into the main branch; approved builds are then bundled into 
a release bundle.  Once the engineering team has finalized the release, whether it be major or minor release, or 
service packs, the executable, along with release notes, are placed in a shared folder by the engineering team on 
the corporate network accessible by Anaqua personnel.  Access to modify source code contained in the version 
control software is restricted to those with a business need.  Release notes are made available to customers to 
decide whether to request implementation of the latest release to their environment.  
  
If a customer elects to enroll in the release, the customer raises a request with the Anaqua support team who then 
creates an internal ticket with the hosting team to implement the release to that customer’s environment.  To 
implement the release on the customer’s environment, the hosting team must be authenticated to the production 
network via RDP and copy the executable from the shared folder on the corporate network to the production network 
and servers.  Separate development, test/QA, and production environments are maintained.  In addition to 
implementing upgrades, Anaqua’s hosting team also run backup requests and scripts requested from the support 
organization.  While customers can be on different releases of the software, there are no separate versions of the 
software for a given customer; every customer on a specific release are using the same codebase.  Furthermore, 
a code review scan is in place to detect critical vulnerabilities for each application release. 
 
Incident Response 
Incident response and escalation policies and procedures are in place to manage unexpected incidents impacting 
the business.  The procedures are reviewed on a periodic basis to help ensure they are still effective in meeting the 
business objectives and include establishing roles and responsibilities for the design, implementation, maintenance, 
and execution of the incident response program.  Incident events can be reported by internal personnel, customers, 
or vendors via an IT request e-mail.  Event coordinators are defined across the various Anaqua regions to help 
ensure that an event response team is assembled to properly assess the event and determine the proper course 
of action.  Events are then triaged for false positives and a help desk ticket that captures the incident, incident 
source, initial severity and priority is created for events that are considered incidents.  Management utilizes a 
ticketing system for documenting, communicating, and collaborating to resolve any identified incidents with 
customers.  Furthermore, the entity monitors infrastructure and software for noncompliance with the standards, 
which could threaten the achievement of Anaqua’s objectives.  Anaqua has also implemented a disaster recovery 
/ business continuity plan to help ensure the required level of continuity for information security during an adverse 
situation.  Corrective measures or changes that occur as a result of incidents and identified deficiencies follow the 
standard change control process. 
 
System Monitoring 
Several monitoring systems, including event logging, are in place to monitor the production environment.  A 
performance monitoring tool is utilized to identify and evaluate ongoing system security threats, changing resource 
utilization needs and unusual system activity and event logs that record user activities, exceptions, faults, and 
information security events are regularly reviewed.  The entity conducts vulnerability scans designed to identify 
potential vulnerabilities or misconfigurations on a weekly basis and engages third-party vendors to perform internal 
penetration and external penetration testing on an annual basis.  Vulnerabilities detected are triaged and monitored 
through resolution.  Information security events are reported to the help desk and tracked through resolution.  
Furthermore, production systems are registered and monitored for antivirus compliance to detect and prevent the 
transmission of data or files that contain certain virus signatures recognized by the antivirus software.

CONTROL ENVIRONMENT 
 
The control environment at Anaqua is the foundation for the other areas of internal control.  It sets the tone of the 
organization and influences the control consciousness of its personnel.  The components of the control environment 
factors include the integrity and ethical values, management’s commitment to competence; its organizational 
structure; the assignment of authority and responsibility; and the oversight and direction provided by the board of 
directors and operations management.

Integrity and Ethical Values 
  
The effectiveness of controls cannot rise above the integrity and ethical values of the people who create, administer, 
and monitor them.  Integrity and ethical values are essential elements of the control environment, affecting the 
design, administration, and monitoring of other components.  Anaqua has established an employee handbook and 
code of conduct which documents the organizational policy statements that communicate workforce conduct 
standards and enforcement procedures.  The policies are reviewed annually and made available to employees via 
the company’s HR portal.  Management requires employees to acknowledge the employee handbook as a 
component of the onboarding process and on an annual basis.  All employees are required to participate in Code 
of Conduct training, and compliance is formally tracked. 

Board of Directors Oversight 
  
Anaqua’s control consciousness is influenced significantly by the board of directors.  The board of directors 
establishes and maintains a formal set of bylaws which describes their responsibilities and oversight of 
management’s system of internal control.  The board of directors has members who are, by majority, independent 
from management and are objective in evaluations and decision making.  On an annual basis, management 
personnel provide the results of external controls assessment reports to the board of directors. 

Organizational Structure and Assignment of Authority and Responsibility 
  
Anaqua’s organizational structure provides the framework within which its activities for achieving entity-wide 
objectives are planned, executed, controlled, and monitored.  Establishing a relevant organizational structure 
includes considering key areas of authority and responsibility and lines of reporting.  Anaqua has developed an 
organizational structure suited to its needs.  Anaqua’s organizational structure depends, in part, on its size and the 
nature of its activities. 
  
This factor includes how authority and responsibility for operating activities are assigned and how reporting 
relationships and authorization hierarchies are established.  It also includes policies relating to business practices, 
knowledge and experience of key personnel, and resources provided for carrying out duties.  Policies and 
communications are in place to help ensure that personnel understand Anaqua’s objectives, know how their 
individual actions interrelate and contribute to those objectives, and recognize how and for what they will be held 
accountable.  A documented organizational chart is in place that defines organizational structures, lines of reporting, 
and areas of authority.  Position descriptions are documented and include the expected behavior and skills needed 
to facilitate the accomplishment of objectives related to the employee’s area of responsibility. 

Commitment to Competence 
  
Competence is the knowledge and skills necessary to accomplish tasks that define employees’ roles and 
responsibilities.  Anaqua’s commitment to competence includes management’s consideration of the competence 
levels for particular jobs and how those levels translate into requisite skills and knowledge.  Anaqua’s commitment 
to competence begins with a background check that is performed, as permitted by local laws, as part of the 
onboarding procedures.  Furthermore, Anaqua has policies and procedures in place that outline the performance 
evaluation process as well as the competency and training requirements for personnel.  Executive management 
has created a training program for its employees, including a data security training for employees upon hire and an 
annual security awareness training thereafter.  Additionally, contractors are required to review and sign the data 
security policy upon hire and are required to attend the annual security awareness training on an annual basis 
thereafter, to understand their obligations and responsibilities to comply with the entity’s security policies.  
Management conducts performance review of employees on an annual basis to evaluate performance of employees 
against expected levels of performance and conduct.

Management establishes accountability by setting a strong tone at the top and holding those accountable for internal 
control responsibilities.  Management communicates the internal control responsibilities and the criteria that

RISK ASSESSMENT 
 
Objective Setting 
  
Management formally documents an organization strategy and objectives that are used to determine entity structure 
and performance metrics.  The objectives documented by executive management are specific, measurable, 
attainable, relevant, and time-bound (SMART).  Further, executive management reviews policies, procedures, and

audits are performed at least annually to demonstrate compliance with relevant laws, regulations, and standards. 

Risk Identification and Analysis 
  
Documented policies and procedures are in place to guide personnel when performing a risk assessment.  
Management has defined a formal risk assessment process that specifies the process for identifying internal and 
external threats and vulnerabilities, evaluating, and addressing risks and defining specified risk tolerances.  A formal 
risk assessment is performed on at least an annual basis to identify internal and external threats and vulnerabilities 
that could impair system commitments and requirements.  Identified risks are rated using a risk evaluation process 
and ratings are approved by the Information Security Management Committee (ISMC), on an annual basis.

Potential for Fraud 
  
On an annual basis, management identifies and assesses the types of fraud (e.g., fraudulent reporting, loss of 
assets, unauthorized system access, overriding controls) that could impact their business and operations.  As part 
of management's assessment of fraud risks, management considers key fraud factors such as incentives, 
pressures, opportunity for unauthorized access or use of data, and employee morale and attitude. 

Risk Mitigation 
  
Once risks have been identified and analyzed, the results are reviewed with management for appropriateness and 
accuracy against the risk acceptance criteria.  Management will then decide how to address risks based on the risk 
assessment score.  Risk treatment options include avoid, mitigate, transfer, and accept.  Risks are then monitored 
to determine the effectiveness of the response plans.  Risk owners are responsible for developing control activities 
to support the achievement of objectives as a result of the risk assessment performed. 
  
Risks can also stem from the use of vendors.  To mitigate vendor risk, prior to onboarding a vendor, a signed vendor 
agreement and non-disclosure agreement is required prior to sharing information designated as confidential with 
third parties.  On an annual basis, management will also collect SOC 2 or similar reports from key vendors for 
review.  A supplier management policy is in place to guide personnel in the new supplier acceptance criteria, new 
suppliers review and acceptance, and existing suppliers review. 
  
Furthermore, the risk management program includes the use of insurance to offset minimize the financial impact of 
loss events that would otherwise impair the ability of the entity to meet its objectives.

MONITORING 
 
Monitoring is a process that assesses the quality of internal control performance over time.  It involves assessing 
the design and operation of controls and taking necessary corrective actions.  This process is accomplished through 
ongoing activities, separate evaluation, or a combination of the two.  Monitoring activities also include using 
information from communications from external parties such as user entity complaints and regulatory comments 
that may indicate problems or highlight areas in need of improvement.  Management has implemented a self-survey 
assessment and compliance program to help ensure the controls are consistently applied as designed.  Additionally, 
management reviews policies, procedures and other control documents for accuracy and applicability on an annual 
basis. 
  
Ongoing Monitoring 
Ongoing monitoring procedures and controls are in place for technical, administrative, and physical safeguards that 
support the control environment.  Monitoring software is used to identify and evaluate ongoing system security 
threats, changing resource utilization needs and unusual system activity.  A formal risk assessment is performed 
on at least an annual basis to identify internal and external threats and vulnerabilities that could impair system 
commitments and requirements.  Furthermore, management engages third parties to perform independent 
assessments of the controls environment annually to assess the effectiveness of controls within the environment. 
  
Separate Evaluations 
Evaluation of an entire internal control system may be prompted by a number of reasons: major strategy or 
management change, major acquisitions or dispositions, or significant changes in operations or methods of 
processing financial information.  Evaluations of internal controls vary in scope and frequency, depending on the 
significance of risks being controlled and importance of the controls in reducing the risks.  Controls addressing 
higher priority risks and those most essential to reducing a given risk will tend to be evaluated more often. 
  
Often evaluations take the form of self-surveys, where persons responsible for a particular unit or function will 
determine the effectiveness of controls for their activities and relay them to management of the company.  These 
assessments are considered by IT management, along with any other internal control evaluations.  Senior 
management is made aware of high-risk vulnerabilities, deviations, and controls gaps identified as part of these 
evaluations.  The findings of these efforts are utilized to help ensure follow-up actions are taken and subsequent 
evaluations are modified, as necessary. 
  
Subservice Organization Monitoring 
Management reviews the third-party attestation reports of subservice organizations on an annual basis to help 
ensure the design and operating effectiveness of controls at the subservice organizations are meeting agreed upon 
expectations.  Additionally, Anaqua is monitoring and assessing the subservice organizations’ services as a part of 
its daily business operations. 

Evaluating and Communicating Deficiencies 
  
Deficiencies in management’s internal control system surface from many sources, including the company’s ongoing 
monitoring procedures, separate evaluations of the internal control system and external parties.  Management has 
developed protocols to help ensure findings of internal control deficiencies are reported.  Deficiencies discovered 
as part of internal evaluations or third-party validation are communicated to management who is responsible for 
developing remediation plans that are recorded and tracked.  Management evaluates the specific facts and 
circumstances related to deficiencies in internal control procedures and make the decision for addressing 
deficiencies based on whether the incident was isolated or requires a change in the company’s procedures or 
personnel.
