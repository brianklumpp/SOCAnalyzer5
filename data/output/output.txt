Management of Deloitte LLP: 
Scope 
We have examined management of Deloitte LLP’s accompanying description of its system titled “Management 
of Deloitte LLP’s Description of its Infrastructure Support Services System” throughout the period October 1, 
2024 to September 30, 2025 (the Description), based on the criteria for a description of a service organization’s 
system in DC section 200, 2018 Description Criteria for a Description of a Service Organization’s System in a 
SOC 2® Report, in AICPA Description Criteria (the Description Criteria), and the suitability of the design and 
operating effectiveness of controls stated in the Description throughout the period October 1, 2024 to 
September 30, 2025 to provide reasonable assurance that Deloitte LLP’s service commitments and system 
requirements were achieved based on the trust services criteria relevant to security and availability (applicable 
trust services criteria) set forth in TSP section 100, 2017 Trust Services Criteria for Security, Availability, 
Processing Integrity, Confidentiality, and Privacy, in AICPA Trust Services Criteria. 
Deloitte Technology Limited is a subservice organization that provides infrastructure support services to 
Deloitte LLP. The Description includes those elements of the infrastructure support services provided to 
Deloitte LLP and the controls designed and operated by Deloitte Technology Limited that are necessary for 
Deloitte LLP to achieve its service commitments and system requirements based on the applicable trust 
services criteria.  
The information included in Section VI, “Other Information Provided by Management of Deloitte LLP”, is 
presented by management of Deloitte LLP to provide additional information and is not a part of the Description. 
Information about Deloitte LLP’s management responses to exceptions identified in the report has not been 
subjected to the procedures applied in the examination of the Description and of the suitability of the design 
and operating effectiveness of controls to achieve Deloitte LLP’s service commitments and system 
requirements based on the applicable trust services criteria and, accordingly, we express no opinion on it. 
The Description indicates that complementary user entity controls that are suitably designed and operating 
effectively are necessary, along with controls at Deloitte LLP and Deloitte Technology Limited, to achieve 
Deloitte LLP’s service commitments and system requirements based on the applicable trust services criteria. 
The Description presents Deloitte LLP and Deloitte Technology Limited’s controls, the applicable trust services 
criteria, and the complementary user entity controls assumed in the design of Deloitte LLP and Deloitte 
Technology Limited’s controls. Our examination did not include such complementary user entity controls, and

The Description is prepared to meet the common needs of a broad range of report users and may not,

SYSTEM OVERVIEW AND SERVICES PROVIDED 
Company Overview 
Services are delivered to clients through certain of the subsidiaries of Deloitte LLP (“DLLP”), including Deloitte & 
Touche LLP, Deloitte Consulting LLP, Deloitte Financial Advisory Services LLP, Deloitte Transactions and Business 
Analytics LLP, and Deloitte Tax LLP (collectively, the “Deloitte U.S. Firms”). DLLP, through its subsidiary, Deloitte 
Services LP, provides various infrastructure, technology, and hosting services through the Deloitte Technology US 
(DT-US) team to the Deloitte U.S. Firms, with the support of Deloitte Technology Limited (DTL), a UK private company 
limited by guarantee, through its Global Deloitte Technology (DT) teams.  
DT-US provides Infrastructure Support Services to the Deloitte U.S. Firms in its on-premises data center located in 
Hermitage, Tennessee. The Hermitage Data Center hosts production network infrastructure and production servers, 
and tools to manage the infrastructure and production network infrastructure that supports these critical shared 
services.  
Subservice Organizations 
DLLP outsources certain infrastructure support services to DTL. Controls executed by DTL on behalf of DLLP related 
to infrastructure are within the scope of this report and are provided through its Global Deloitte Technology (DT) 
teams. 
Scope of this Report 
The scope of this report includes Infrastructure Support Services for client-facing applications that are hosted in 
DLLP’s data center located in Hermitage, Tennessee. The infrastructure environment does not include servers 
hosting applications for DLLP’s internal or back-office use or laptops outside of the Windows operating system 
environment. 
The scope of this report also includes the support services provided by DLLP’s India based subsidiaries (“U.S. India” 
or “USI”) in Hyderabad, Bengaluru, Mumbai Gurgaon, Pune, Chennai, and Kolkata and DLLP’s Mexico based 
subsidiaries (“U.S. Mexico” or “USM”) which have offices in Queretaro. While these locations do not house servers, 
they do house network equipment and support professionals.  
DLLP utilizes DTL for certain infrastructure support services. The scope of this report excludes the following: 
• 
Applications or databases

Systems or services used by DT-US for back-office purposes

Software 
DLLP’s tools/systems listed under the heading “Infrastructure” support the Windows and Linux operating system 
software. 
The professionals of DLLP and the Deloitte U.S. Firms access the tools/systems through their organization-supplied 
laptops either by connecting to the network in offices or through a VPN gateway using Advanced Encryption 
Standard (AES) 256-bit.  
Modern Access Control (MAC) is the name for DLLP’s Identity Governance and Administration platform based on 
SailPoint’s IdentityIQ software. MAC empowers business users with automated access requests and provides access 
right owner the ability to review access membership for appropriateness on a quarterly. 
Secret Server is a centrally managed privileged account management solution that protects DLLP’s privileged 
accounts. Secret Server manages privileged accounts by requiring standard users to pass MFA to gain access to Secret 
Server. Secret Server only allows access to privileged accounts each user has access. If a privileged account is checked 
out (used) then Secret Server will manage the privileged account and rotate the password upon check-in.  
Configuration Management Database (CMDB) is an inventory tool for assets. CMDB pulls data from assets utilizing 
periodic probes every two to six hours depending on the environment and is integrated with event feeds to update 
asset information in real time.

Compass is a reporting/repository tool that pulls data from Omni for reporting purposes. It is configured to pull data 
directly from Omni and store it in the Compass database. Then, application programming interfaces (APIs) are used 
to pull the data from the Compass database and display the data in the Compass dashboard as a report.  
Splunk is a security monitoring and data analysis tool that provides comprehensive security monitoring, threat 
detection, and incident response capabilities. Splunk collects logs, which contain events, from various technologies 
that represent the IT environment. Logs are collected from Splunk Forwarding agents and/or collection servers 
around the DLLP network. Splunk then uses threat detection rules to monitor, detect, and alert on unusual and 
potentially malicious behavior. Once a Splunk alert is triggered, it is automatically fed into a ServiceNow ticket. 
SolarWinds is a network performance monitoring tool that provides comprehensive monitoring of network devices, 
including routers, switches, and firewalls. It provides monitoring capabilities for health and performance metrics.  
Dynatrace is a comprehensive software intelligence platform that provides advanced availability monitoring and 
performance management for the infrastructure, including servers and VMs. Dynatrace leverages artificial 
intelligence (AI) to provide performance metrics and perform automatic root cause analysis to identify the 
underlying causes of any performance issues.

Control Environment 
Board of Directors 
The control environment of DLLP is influenced by the Board of Directors (“Board”) and its seven committees. Board 
committees operate on behalf of the Board, as stewards of and specialists in particular areas, working with 
management and the full Board. Board committees are required to formally report to the Board on their activities, 
including issues of internal control within DLLP. 
Board member expertise and experience include an understanding of the organization's business operations, 
strategic initiatives, and major transactions, and may present alternate views. 
Integrity and Ethical Values 
The Board of DLLP has adopted a Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct (“Code”) that serves as a guide for actions 
and behaviors to be followed by its professionals. 
The Code defines the expectations for all DLLP professionals, including their obligation to report any potential 
violations, and documents a sanctions policy for those who violate the Code. All professionals are required to know, 
understand and comply with the Code, both in letter and in spirit. 
Professionals receive periodic reminders to comply with the Code and must complete a review of the Code as a part 
of their annual independence representation. 
Ethics and Compliance Department – Board of Directors and Senior Leadership  
The Chief Ethics and Compliance Officer designs and provides reports to the Board on compliance matters and 
organizes annual meetings with the senior leadership team for their compliance review. 
Ethics and Compliance Hotline 
A confidential and anonymous Ethics and Compliance Hotline is available for DLLP professionals to report issues. The 
hotline is accessible 24x7 by phone, online, or via mobile device. The individual may also send a letter reporting the 
concern to Chief Ethics and Compliance Officer. 
Professionals are instructed that it is their duty to promptly report any concerns of suspected or known violations of 
the Code or other policies or guidelines of DLLP. The procedures to be followed for making such a report are outlined 
on the Ethics and Compliance intranet page. DLLP professionals are also encouraged to communicate the issue to 
their manager, their manager’s manager, their HR contact, or the Office of Compliance or Integrity Helpline. 
Internal and External Reporting Objectives 
DLLP communicates its objectives and changes to objectives (i.e., technology changes, leadership changes, and policy 
changes) through channels such as a post on DLLP’s intranet site or email.

Training 
DLLP provides compliance and role-based training opportunities to all professionals. Independence, ethics, and 
confidentiality training is required of all DLLP professionals. The independence training includes an anti-corruption 
section that focuses on DLLP’s anti-corruption policies and highlights policies that reinforce the need for DLLP 
professionals to work with integrity and comply with the anti-corruption laws of the countries in which DLLP 
operates. Active DLLP professionals are required to complete these training courses and compliance with all 
mandatory courses is tracked on an ongoing basis. 
Accountability 
DLLP professionals are accountable for understanding and adhering to the guidance in the DLLP Cyber Security 
policy, any applicable supporting standards, and all Administrative Policy Releases (APRs). Any individual not 
employed by DLLP (e.g., visitors accessing the guest network, contractors or vendors accessing the DLLP VPN), but 
allowed to access, manage, or process information assets of DLLP, is also accountable for understanding and 
adhering to the guidance in the Cyber Security policy and standards. Contractors are required to sign an agreement 
that establishes standards of conduct regarding security, confidentiality, privacy, personally identifiable information 
(PII), and the disclosure of such information. 
Commitment to Competence 
The hiring managers of DLLP define appropriate job requirements prior to recruiting, interviewing, and hiring for a 
position of employment. Job requirements include the primary responsibilities and tasks involved in the job, the 
background needed to perform the job, and personal characteristics required. Minimum education and prior work 
experience are also documented as part of the hiring requirements. These areas are documented for professionals 
responsible for designing, developing, implementing, operating, maintaining, and monitoring the security and 
availability of the system. Once the requirements have been determined, managers create a job description, which

Logical Access Management 
Network Access Controls 
DLLP has implemented industry standard Network Access Controls (NAC) to prevent unauthorized devices from 
accessing the DLLP network. The NAC and group policy settings enforce compliance checks and authentication of 
DLLP managed devices for wired and wireless access. A firewall and intrusion prevention system (IPS) is employed 
at the point of entry to the DLLP network and between various security zones within the DLLP network. Access 
control lists are placed on firewalls controlling the inbound and outbound flow of traffic. Traffic is denied by default 
unless approved by the gateway protocols as configured and approved by the DLLP security team. A demilitarized 
zone (DMZ) and trusted zones are used to segment traffic to areas that are protected in accordance with the 
accepted risk levels. Web servers located on a publicly reachable network segment are separated from the internal 
network by a firewall (i.e., DMZ). DLLP's wireless network is encrypted and restricted to DLLP-managed devices. 
Access Control – Network Appliances and Operating System 
The production environment is logically segregated from non-production through separate non-production (i.e., 
development, staging) and production servers for each system.  
Access to DLLP information contained on DLLP IT systems is granted on the principle of least privilege and need-to-
know basis and is approved by the DLLP data owner. DLLP has policies and procedures in place to manage user 
accounts and access for new hires, existing staff, transfers, and terminated professionals. Automated processes link 
the Human Resource (HR) system to administer user access. Appropriate vendor and contractor access is granted on 
a temporary basis via a formal approval procedure. Upon approval, the vendor user accounts are created in a 
controlled domain organizational unit giving the access necessary to perform their defined duties. Vendor and 
contractor access are granted on a temporary basis based on business need. Remote access is provided via Transport 
Layer Security (TLS) using two-factor authentication with account activity being logged to DLLP’s logging/alerting 
mechanism. Depending on the level and type of access required, a Virtual Private Network (VPN) solution provides 
a secure virtual session or web interface with access into the needed application(s) or platform. For all web-based 
applications (including VPN), Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) has been enabled. Verification options include text 
message or mobile application. End users are assigned unique user IDs for access to the network. User accounts are 
disabled after 90 days of inactivity and deleted upon an additional 30 days of inactivity. Windows AD accounts on 
the primary domain are disabled using Microsoft Identity Manager (MIM) via an automated process on the day of 
termination. Once deleted from the network, a user ID cannot be reused for two years. Upon a user’s job transfer, 
management uses the MAC tool to de-provision access that is no longer needed. Upon marking a user to be 
deprovisioned, the MAC tool automatically runs a job to de-provision users. In the event that a failure occurs, a ticket 
is opened to manually de-provision the access. Failed deprovisioned transactions are also reviewed weekly, and 
action is taken to resolve the failed transactions timely. 
Privileged access requires a separate privileged user account, which is only used for administrative activities and not 
used for other general user activities. Privileged user accounts are established and administered in accordance with 
a role-based access scheme that organizes all system and network privileges into role-based groups (e.g., key 
management, network, system administration, database administration, and web administration). 
The privileged account provisioning process requires approvals from a user’s supervisor as well as data owners. 
Privileged access is managed by a privileged access management solution (PAM) that requires a user to be on the 
DLLP network and authenticate using multi-factor authentication (MFA). The tool also requires the user to check out 
the privileged account password, which is randomly generated every 24 hours. Privileged accounts are disabled after 
30 days of inactivity and are removed manually within two days of termination. Privileged account activities are 
logged, logs are stored in a centralized log management solution, and activities are subject to review based on the 
rules defined within the SIEM tool.

Cyber Security Incident Management

Change Management 
DLLP has a change management process in place for its IT systems, including changes to the network, firewalls, and 
servers. This change management process has standardized methods, processes, and procedures that facilitate 
efficient and prompt handling of changes, including maintaining the proper balance between the need for the 
change and the potential detrimental impact of the change. Relevant changes are documented, tested, and 
approved prior to implementation in production. Each change request documents the change plan, impact 
assessment, backout plan, and post-implementation verification. Emergency changes follow a similar process; 
however, testing and approval may be documented within seven business days after the implementation. 
Changes are reviewed and approved by the Change Advisory Board (CAB) prior to implementation. The CAB reviews 
these changes during regularly scheduled meetings, which include the CTO and the members of DLLP responsible 
for the security and availability commitments and requirements. Approved changes are tested. Deployment 
windows are scheduled to deploy non-emergency changes during non-peak hours.  
Access to implement changes to the production environment is appropriately restricted. Changes are communicated 
to appropriate data owners. Changes that affect system user responsibilities or DLLP’s commitments and 
requirements relevant to security and availability are communicated through email messages to impacted users as 
well as alerts posted to the internal professional’s site. 
Patch Management

For each monthly patch cycle, a change request is documented and approved. The patches are first applied to a 
group of test servers before being deployed to production. Server patches are implemented within three months of 
the vendor release. Emergency patch management meetings are convened as necessary.

whole-disk encryption and professionals are required to use DLLP-approved encryption methods when transmitting 
confidential data. WinZip and Rights Management are installed on all DLLP-issued laptops. These encryption 
methods are FIPS 140-2 compliant. In addition, authentication credentials transmitted to network devices are 
encrypted in transit. Laptops are configured to block the use of removable media.  
Mobile devices must have the mobile device management (MDM) software installed and be enrolled in the MDM 
solution to access the DLLP network. The MDM software encrypts the device, isolates, and protects DLLP data, 
requires a PIN, enforces screen lockout after inactivity, enforces operating system updates, and remotely wipes the 
device after 10 incorrect PIN attempts or if the device is reported lost or stolen.

procedures, and applicable legal, statutory, or regulatory compliance obligations. The policy is updated annually. 
Official records are archived and retained in the records management system in accordance with the Records

Data Sharing 
The Data Classification and Handling policy defines all non-public client data as confidential. Confidential information 
is not shared with third parties unless a confidentiality agreement has been signed by the recipient. Access to 
confidential data is provisioned only after the data owner’s approval. 
Data Backup and Recovery 
Production servers are scheduled for a daily incremental backup. If the backup fails for any reason, it will send out 
an alert and automatically retry to backup three times. If the backup fails more than four times, an incident ticket is 
opened in ServiceNow and tracked for resolution.  
In the normal operation of the data center, disk-based backup restores are performed multiple times per week. 
These restores validate the integrity of backup data at rest on disk. Media assets (laptops or hard drives) that are 
unable to be wiped by DLLP are sent to a third party for destruction, and DLLP tracks and stores the destruction 
report as proof that the media asset was destroyed. Backup data stored on disk is encrypted using AES-256 
standards.  
Disaster Recovery/Business Continuity Management 
While the goal of the overall Business Continuity Management program is to reduce the likelihood of a disruption, 
DLLP has developed and implemented a DR/Business Continuity Program (BCP) that enables the recovery of DLLP 
managed IT services so that the end-to-end business process can continue should a disruption occur. DLLP’s program 
includes the following basic activities: 
• 
Conducting a business impact assessment to help define criticality of processes and systems related to recovery 
time objective. Critical technology services are defined as Platinum, Gold, Siver or Tier 0, Tier 1, Tier 2 and Tier 
3. 
• 
Disaster recovery planning of our technology through multiple recovery capabilities. 
• 
Performing a risk assessment for each of the DLLP Managed IT services to identify the business criticality, service 
dependencies and supporting service resilience architecture.  
• 
Evaluating the options for recovery; producing a contingency/DR plan for critical technology services; and 
testing, reviewing, and revising that contingency plan on a regular basis. 
• 
Business continuity test plans for DLLP Managed IT services are scoped and the problems identified are 
documented with an associated remediation action. The BCP or DR plan is reviewed and approved annually and 
tested on an annual basis as needed. A Business Impact Assessment (BIA) is performed each fiscal year to 
identify threats to software, hardware, people, and physical assets. 
• 
Internal review process for maintaining the quality of plans and services. 
• 
For server infrastructure that is administered by DT-US, a BCP/DR plan is in place for technical services using the 
server. 
• 
The BCP and plans include emergency-response business procedures, which go into effect following the 
occurrence of a disaster or other unplanned interruption. 
• 
DR plans include technical and business contact call lists, as well as notification and escalation information and 
architecture diagrams. Where pertinent, third-party information is also included. Recovery time objectives and 
recovery point objectives are documented and tested for each plan. 
• 
Risk assessment test scenarios are based on impacts that include loss of the service and loss of data center 
strategy. Test results are reviewed and recorded.
